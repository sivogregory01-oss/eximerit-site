What sits behind a result like this

What we see most

How a quality system gets out of control.

Records that stay open.
Deviations, CAPAs and change controls run past their target dates. Each one looks small. An inspector reads the overdue list first.
A team that does not grow with the work.
New products, sites and volumes arrive, and the same few people review the batch records. The know-how sits with them, and leaves when they do.
Procedures that describe last year.
The SOP shows what was done for the last audit, and the floor works differently.
Readiness by effort.
The weeks before an inspection go on catching up, and the effort stops when the inspection does.

Two of our projects are built for this: QMS stabilisation and the quality team scalability review.

What comes out when it is fixed

What can come out

The same evidence, in less time.

Over 60% of quality professionals lose about a quarter of their working day to administration. That time is what comes back.

Reviewing a batch record typically takes 4 to 8 hours when the data is clean. In reported cases it takes under an hour a batch, and a qualified reviewer still signs off every record.

We count the hours released before and after: a month for the team, a batch for the handling of documentation.

  • 40 to 60%less time on deviation and CAPA administration
  • 50 to 60%less time to assess and approve a change
  • About 70%less manual effort reviewing batch records
  • 40 to 90%less time preparing product quality reviews
  • 30 to 50%less validation effort
  • 20 to 30%fewer stability samples tested

Ranges reported in published case studies, mostly from single sites and software vendors. Your result depends on your products, records and volume, and we size it before anything is agreed. Sources on request.

What that is worth to a team like yours

The business case

Take the admin out of quality and the bottleneck moves.

The problem.
When quality is seen as the limit on the business, much of the cause is admin: chasing records, re-keying data, filing and reporting. The queue grows, and the team that could clear it is busy feeding it.
The proposal.
A fixed-scope project led by a partner. We find where the admin hours go, take them out, and measure the hours and the cost of quality before and after. The result and the fee are agreed in writing before we start.
The return.
Hours back for the quality team, shorter release and closure times, and a function that carries more volume without a matching rise in headcount.

The name

Exceptional by merit.

Eximerit joins two words. Eximius is Latin for exceptional: taken out from the rest. Merit is what you have earned by what you have done.

In a GMP plant, claims are checked against records. A batch is released on its documentation, a deviation is closed on evidence, and an inspector asks to see the work. We hold ourselves to the same test: judge us by the result.
